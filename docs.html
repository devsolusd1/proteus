CHAPTER I

The name.

Proteus is the Old Man of the Sea: a minor god in Homer's Odyssey who herds Poseidon's seals and knows everything — the past, the present, the way home. There is only one problem with asking him. He will not answer. Ask, and he changes shape.

He became a lion, a serpent, a leopard, a boar; then running water; then a tall tree in leaf. And still they held him. AFTER HOMER, ODYSSEY IV — MENELAUS ON THE ISLAND OF PHAROS

In the fourth book, Menelaus is stranded on Pharos on his way home from Troy. Proteus's own daughter tells him the trick: ambush the god at noon, seize him, and hold on. He will become every terrible thing he can think of. Do not let go. When he runs out of shapes and returns to his own, he must speak — and he must tell the truth.

That is the whole token. A name that will not sit still, a god that anyone may seize, and a prize that goes only to whoever holds on. English still keeps him in a word: protean, able to take many forms.

Proteus or Protheus?

The engraving on the front page reads PROTHEVS. That is not a typo — it is sixteenth-century Latin, where V stood for U and an extra H was a fashionable way to make a Greek name look more Greek. The Greek itself is Πρωτεύς: no theta, no H. The token is Proteus. The old spelling stays on the print, where it belongs.

CHAPTER III

The Rite of Capture.

Anyone holding $PROTEUS may perform the Rite. It has three parts, and the contract enforces all of them.

1 · The tribute

To seize the god you burn $PROTEUS — send it to the void, permanently. The amount is the current tribute, shown live on the front page. Each successful capture multiplies the next tribute by 1.4, so the crown gets more expensive with every hand that has held it. Nothing burned ever returns: the supply shrinks with every form he takes.

2 · The struggle

The burn does not rename him on the spot. For 480 blocks — about two minutes on Robinhood Chain — the capture is sealed: the new name, ticker and face are locked in but not yet shown, and no rival can start another capture. The delay exists so that no one can flip the name in the same breath as a trade. Everyone sees the struggle; no one can snipe it.

3 · The reign

When the last block passes, the god yields. The contract writes the new name, ticker and face, and the captor's address becomes the Captor: from that block on, half of the tithe on every trade streams to it in ETH. There is no term. The reign ends only when someone else burns the next tribute and holds on through their own struggle.

CHAPTER IV

The tithe.

Every trade of $PROTEUS pays a 2% tithe, split down the middle: one percent to the Temple, one percent to the Captor. The Captor's share is paid continuously, trade by trade, in ETH — there is no prize to claim and nothing to stake. Until the very first capture there is no Captor, and the full tithe rests with the Temple.

What the Temple does with its half

The Temple is the founding team's treasury. Its share keeps the pool deep, funds the rituals — this site, the art, the events around each new form — and pays for the development of the contract and the tools around it.

CHAPTER VI

Questions.

The name changed. Did my tokens change?

No. Same contract, same balance, same liquidity — only the metadata moved. If your wallet still shows the old ticker, it has cached it; the address is what matters.

Can I capture him more than once?

As often as you can pay. Each capture costs 1.4× the last one, whoever performed it.

Can someone capture him back immediately?

Only by burning the next, larger tribute and sitting through their own 480-block struggle. While a struggle is running, no one can start another.

Is the tribute refundable?

No. The burn happens first and is ash the moment the transaction confirms. There is no abandoning a capture — only the struggle, then the reign.

Where does the Captor's ETH come from?

From the tithe on each trade, streamed to the Captor's address as trades happen. Nothing to claim, nothing to stake, nothing to be front-run.

What if nobody captures him for a long time?

The reign simply continues and the Captor keeps collecting. The tribute stays where the last capture left it.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a myth with a contract attached. Read the contract, mind the tithe, and never burn what you cannot afford to lose.
